# Midterms × crypto — thread (video in the first post)

1/ (video)
crypto spent ~$200M on the US midterms and still ended up with a democratic congress

if you don't follow US politics, this thread explains what happened and why it matters for anyone in crypto

2/
the midterms are the election halfway through a president's term. Americans vote for Congress, which is the part of the government that actually writes the laws

whoever controls Congress decides which crypto bills move and which ones die

3/
crypto went in big this time. the industry has its own super PAC called Fairshake, a political fund that can raise unlimited money to back or attack candidates

together with a few smaller ones, they spent around $200M trying to get crypto-friendly people elected

4/
it mostly didn't work. democrats took both the House and the Senate

the race everyone watched was Ohio. Sherrod Brown has been one of crypto's loudest critics for years, tens of millions were spent to keep him out, and he won anyway

5/
the CLARITY Act is the bill that would finally give crypto clear rules in the US: which tokens count as securities, which count as commodities, and who regulates what

the industry was counting on it passing soon. with this Congress it will probably stall or come back heavily rewritten

6/
Elizabeth Warren is expected to chair the Senate Banking Committee, the group that decides which financial laws even get a vote

she has pushed for tighter rules on exchanges, stablecoins and KYC for years, so expect a lot more pressure there

7/
the market didn't love it either. BTC hit ~$80k on election night and was back around $75k two days later, and Trump-linked tokens fell the hardest

8/
people on X are split. some call it a reset, some say a divided government at least keeps things from swinging too far, and some think Warren is about to go after the whole industry

9/
the general read: regulation isn't going away, it's just slower and stricter now. if you're building or marketing a crypto project in the US, compliance is about to become part of the pitch
