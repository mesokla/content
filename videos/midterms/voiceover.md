# Midterms × crypto — voiceover script (English, ~73 s)

Numbers and abbreviations are written the way they should be spoken, so ElevenLabs reads them correctly.

## Full text (paste into ElevenLabs as one piece)

Crypto spent two hundred million dollars on the US midterms... and lost.

On November third and fourth, Democrats took control of both the House and the Senate.

Crypto super PACs, mostly Fairshake, poured in up to two hundred million dollars. The key races didn't go their way.

The biggest blow came in Ohio. Long-time crypto sceptic Sherrod Brown is back in the Senate, despite tens of millions spent against him.

And it gets tougher. Elizabeth Warren is expected to chair the Senate Banking Committee. That means more pressure on exchanges, stablecoins, A-M-L and K-Y-C rules, and on the crypto business of public officials.

Market structure bills like the CLARITY Act will likely stall, or be heavily rewritten.

The market couldn't decide. A dip during the count, Bitcoin back above seventy-eight thousand on Wednesday, then a heavier sell-off. Trump-linked tokens fell the hardest.

On X, the takes split three ways. It's a reset, not the end. Gridlock protects us from extremes. Or: Warren's anti-crypto army is coming.

My take? Regulation isn't dead. It's slower, and stricter. Compliance becomes part of the pitch. Nobody should be promising clarity soon. And political tokens carry political risk.

So what's your take? Tell me in the replies.
